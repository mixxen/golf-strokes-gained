Comparison baseline

PGA TOUR — Broadie 2003–2010

Every stroke is compared with the expected number of strokes a PGA TOUR player needed to hole out from the same lie and distance in Mark Broadie’s published ShotLink benchmark.

Historical · unadjusted

The calculation

A shot gains strokes when it improves the golfer’s expected position by more than the one stroke it cost to play.

SG = J(start lie, distance) − 1 − J(finish lie, distance)

J is the expected number of strokes needed to hole out. A penalty adds another stroke to the cost. A standard stroke-and-distance result is therefore exactly −2.00 SG.

Benchmark and lie mapping

Off-green values come from Broadie’s Table B.1, estimated from more than eight million PGA TOUR ShotLink strokes from 2003–2010. Putting uses his published PGA TOUR putting table.

What the category totals mean

The app reports off-the-tee, approach, around-the-green, and putting SG. These labels only decide which subtotal receives a shot; they do not change the shot’s SG calculation.

PGA TOUR defines around the green as a shot played off the putting surface within 30 yards of the green’s edge. The app currently infers this category using distance to the flag because green-edge geometry is not yet available. This conservative rule can place some official around-the-green shots in the approach subtotal, but it does not change total SG.

Important limitations

  • The benchmark is historical and is not the PGA TOUR’s unpublished current lookup surface.
  • Results are not adjusted for event, course, round, weather, or field strength.
  • The public off-green table starts at 10 yards, so shorter results use that endpoint.
  • Fringe uses a documented fairway proxy because the public benchmark has no separate fringe state.
  • Green-edge distance or an explicit category override remains a future improvement.
